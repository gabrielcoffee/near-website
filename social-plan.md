# Near — 14-day posting plan (Sep 8 – Sep 20, 2026)

Cadence: every other day, 7 posts, same day on both platforms. Twitter carries the media; LinkedIn carries the story.

**LinkedIn posts are written in Portuguese. Twitter posts are written in English.** Every LinkedIn body below is the real post, ready to paste. No English version in the main body, ever.

## Platform rules

**Twitter (English)**
- 1 to 3 lines. Media every time: video > GIF > screenshot. Never text-only.
- No hashtags. Link goes in the first reply, never the main tweet.
- Show the thing. Let the video do the explaining.

**LinkedIn (Portuguese, pt-BR)**
- First 2 lines are the hook (everything before "ver mais"). Make them stand alone.
- One idea per post. Short paragraphs, one sentence each. Lots of white space.
- End with a question, not a pitch. Link in the first comment.
- No hashtags, or max 3 at the very bottom.
- Same media as Twitter, but a still frame or a shorter cut.
- Tone: como você fala, não como um post de LinkedIn. Primeira pessoa, informal, "tô" e "pra" liberados. Sem "estou animado para compartilhar", sem emoji de foguete, sem lição de vida no final.

## Language: profile in English, LinkedIn in Portuguese, Twitter in English

Two different things get confused here. Which language you post in, and which language brings recruiter messages.

**Inbound messages from foreign companies come from your profile, not your posts.** Recruiters find people through LinkedIn Recruiter search, which reads structured fields: headline, job titles, skills, location, years of experience. It does not care what language your last post was in. That is why people who post only in Portuguese still get approached by companies abroad, especially US and European companies that hire in Brazil on purpose. So the profile has to be in English: headline, about, every job title and description, skills. Add a Portuguese profile version through LinkedIn's second-language option.

**What you post in changes who sees the post, not who can find you.** Both feeds show a post first to people who already engage with you. Your network is Brazilian, so a Portuguese post gets real engagement and an English post to that same network gets less. Engagement is what buys reach, so early on Portuguese usually performs better on LinkedIn, and a post that performs raises your profile views across the board.

The split:

- **LinkedIn: Portuguese.** Always, body included. Your network is here, engagement is easier, and it feeds profile views. Recruiters abroad reach you through the English profile anyway. Revisit only if the goal becomes an international audience rather than international messages.
- **Twitter: English.** No existing network to lean on, so there is nothing to lose, and the indie hacker and product crowd who would care about this reads English. Portuguese there caps you inside a small pt-BR bubble with almost no company presence.

Optional: paste the English version of a LinkedIn post in the first comment, under the link, when a post is worth showing to people abroad.

Never mix both languages in one post body. It halves readability and both feeds read it as low quality.

## The name

The app is called **Near** on the site and in every post. Post #1 frames it as provisional ("por enquanto vou chamar de"), which leaves room to change it and invites feedback without making the post about the name. Lock it for good before post #5, where the tech detail makes a rename expensive.

### 2 — Thu Sep 10 — The handshake

**Twitter (EN)**

This is the entire friend request flow.

Stand next to someone. Both tap. 30 seconds.

No usernames. No QR codes. No "people you may know".

*(video: two phones, bubbles appear, both tap, match animation)*
*Reply: "Works over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, so it doesn't even need internet to see who's in the room."*

**LinkedIn (PT)**

O meu app não tem pedido de amizade.
Isso aqui tomou o lugar.

Pra virar amigo de alguém, vocês dois precisam estar na mesma sala.

Seu celular fica procurando outros celulares por perto, em silêncio. Quando acha um, aparece uma bolha na tela.

Você toca na bolha da pessoa. A pessoa toca na sua. Se os dois toques acontecem em até 30 segundos, pronto, vocês são amigos.

É isso. Sem busca, sem @, sem pedido parado numa aba, sem estranho nenhum.

Isso também quer dizer que o app não cresce do jeito que app social costuma crescer. Sem convite em cadeia, sem "encontre seus contatos", sem pessoa sugerida.

E esse é justamente o ponto. Todo amigo ali é alguém do lado de quem você já esteve.

Você baixaria uma rede social que não tem barra de busca?

Sem algoritmo, sem anúncio, sem IA. Seu feed são seus amigos, do mais novo pro mais antigo. Ninguém pagou pra estar ali e nada ali foi gerado.

Tirar funcionalidade é bem mais difícil que colocar. Cada uma dessas foi uma conversa de "mas e se o usuário quiser..." comigo mesmo.

O único upgrade: um feed de vinte pessoas que você ama em vez de duas mil que você nem conhece.
