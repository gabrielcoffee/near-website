# Near — 14-day posting plan (Sep 7 – Sep 20, 2026)

Cadence: every other day, 7 posts, same day on both platforms. Twitter carries the media; LinkedIn carries the story.

## Platform rules

**Twitter**
- 1 to 3 lines. Media every time: video > GIF > screenshot. Never text-only.
- No hashtags. Link goes in the first reply, never the main tweet.
- Show the thing. Let the video do the explaining.

**LinkedIn**
- First 2 lines are the hook (everything before "see more"). Make them stand alone.
- One idea per post. Short paragraphs, one sentence each. Lots of white space.
- End with a question, not a pitch. Link in the first comment.
- No hashtags, or max 3 at the very bottom.
- Same media as Twitter, but a still frame or a shorter cut.

### 2 — Wed Sep 9 — The handshake

**Twitter**

> This is the entire friend request flow in Near.
>
> Stand next to someone. Both tap. 30 seconds.
>
> No usernames. No QR codes. No "people you may know".

*(video: two phones, bubbles appear, both tap, match animation)*
*Reply: "Works over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, so it doesn't even need internet to see who's in the room."*

**LinkedIn**

> Near has no friend requests.
> Here's what replaced them.
>
> To become friends, two people have to be in the same room.
>
> Your phone quietly looks for other phones nearby. When it finds one, a bubble shows up.
>
> You tap the bubble. They tap yours. If both taps happen within 30 seconds, you're friends.
>
> That's it. No search bar. No requests waiting in a tab. No strangers.
>
> It also means the app cannot grow the way social apps usually grow. No invite loops, no "find your contacts", no suggested people.
>
> That's the point. Every friend on Near is someone you've actually stood next to.
>
> Would you download a social app that has no search?

*(video: same two-phone clip, or one still of the match moment)*

**LinkedIn**

> My app's pitch is "Instagram, but worse."
> On purpose.
>
> Everything you're used to is missing, and each one is missing for a reason.
>
> No algorithm, no ads, no AI slop. Your feed is your friends, newest first. Nobody paid to be there, nothing in it was generated.
> No likes. If you care, you write a comment. Words, not hearts.
> No highlight reel. Instagram is everyone's best day, filtered. Near is a Tuesday.
> No endless scroll. The feed ends when your friends stop posting. Then you go outside.
> No FOMO. You only see people you actually see. If it's not on Near, you were probably there.
>
> Removing features is harder than adding them. Every one of these was a "but what if users want..." conversation with myself.
>
> The one upgrade: a feed of twenty people you love instead of two thousand you don't.
>
> Which of these would you miss the most?
